# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Conference and event visitors at a Vexl booth who are curious about Bitcoin privacy, irritated by KYC, or ready to try peer-to-peer trading. They use the experience standing at a 43-inch 16:9 Full HD capacitive touchscreen kiosk, usually alone, in a public event environment with limited attention and a line potentially forming behind them.

The interaction should feel quick, physical, and booth-native: large tap targets, short copy, immediate feedback, and a clear path from diagnosis to opening Vexl, scanning the QR code, creating or reacting to one offer, and getting a booth stamp.

## Product Purpose

This is a playful Vexl privacy clinic that turns KYC frustration into a short diagnostic ritual. Visitors select the exchange and privacy symptoms they recognize, receive a satirical prescription, and are nudged toward the real treatment: using Vexl for private, people-to-people Bitcoin through friends and friends of friends.

Success means a visitor understands why Vexl is relevant, laughs or recognizes themselves in the diagnosis, scans the download QR code, and takes one concrete booth action before leaving.

## Brand Personality

Playful, underground, direct.

The voice is clinic satire with a privacy-first edge: symptoms, prescriptions, diagnoses, stamps, and treatment plans, but never generic wellness copy. It should feel like a booth artifact from a parallel-economy clinic, not a corporate fintech quiz.

## Anti-references

Do not make this look like generic fintech, exchange onboarding, a banking kiosk, a medical portal, or glossy crypto advertising. Avoid blue corporate gradients, stock trading imagery, bank-card metaphors, candlestick-chart hero graphics, 3D coins, over-serious compliance language, and generic SaaS explainer cards.

Avoid interaction patterns that slow a booth visitor down: small controls, long reading, hover-dependent UI, dense forms, multi-step account-style onboarding, or anything that assumes a mouse and keyboard.

## Design Principles

1. Make the booth interaction obvious from three steps away. The first screen should read instantly on a 43-inch display and invite touch without explanation.
2. Turn KYC pain into a memorable ritual. The clinic metaphor should create a smile, then move quickly to the Vexl action.
3. Keep every step stamp-worthy. The flow should end in a concrete booth action: scan, install, create or react to one offer, ask for the stamp.
4. Preserve Vexl's social privacy story. Emphasize P2P Bitcoin, friends of friends, no KYC, encrypted chat, open source, and identity revealed only by choice.
5. Design for public use by one person at a time. Prioritize large targets, readable type, forgiving reset behavior, and fast comprehension over web-page density.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target practical kiosk accessibility rather than a formal compliance level. Use high contrast, large text, large tap areas, clear selected states, and no reliance on hover. Keep motion purposeful and optional enough that the interaction remains understandable if animation is reduced or absent.

Because visitors may be standing, distracted, or unfamiliar with Vexl, the experience should avoid tiny labels, long paragraphs, low-contrast gray text, and color-only state changes.
